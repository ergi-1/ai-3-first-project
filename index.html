1. Theoretical Rationale

Why Agent-Based Modeling (ABM) for Epidemics?

Traditional Models (SIR / Differential)

  • Assumes homogenous populations (everyone has same transmission risk).
  • Assumes perfect mixing (no spatial segregation or local clusters).
  • Incapable of modeling individual behavioral changes (e.g. self-quarantine, vaccine hesitancy).

EpiSim-ABM Approach

  • Demographic Heterogeneity: Unique agent attributes (Age, Health, Compliances, Hesitancy).
  • Spatial Heterogeneity: Clear diurnal commuting routines between Homes, Offices, and Parks.
  • Policy Responsiveness: Agents respond behaviorally to mask mandates and lockdown rules dynamically.

Applied AI - Academic Project Rationale Report

EpiSim-ABM: Evaluating Spatial Public Health Interventions via Complex Agent-Based Spatial Modeling

Course Assignment: Applied AI Course Department of Computer Science & Intelligent Systems

Abstract

Mathematical modeling of infectious diseases has traditionally relied on compartmental differential equations (e.g., standard SIR models), which operate under assumptions of homogeneous mixing and static population characteristics. This paper presents EpiSim-ABM, an Agent-Based Model written from first principles in high-performance JavaScript to simulate the spatial spreading of an aerosol pathogen inside a bounded municipal ecosystem. By structuring the spatial boundaries into specific household, workplace, and leisure nodes, and scheduling diurnal commuting trajectories, we analyze how heterogeneous agent demographics (age profiles, mask compliance indices, vaccine hesitancy) lead to emergent macroscopic infectious curves. We evaluate three health policy scenarios: uncontained spread, threshold-based dynamic lockdowns, and proactive targeted vaccination campaigns, demonstrating that agent behavioral compliance scales non-linearly with containment outcomes, providing a richer, context-aware policy tool than traditional epidemiological formulas.

1. Introduction & Problem Definition

Epidemic control represents a complex, non-linear system where micro-level interactions between individuals generate macro-level patterns of viral spread. In standard compartment models, a population is divided into susceptible, infectious, and recovered pools, with transitions governed by system-wide rates (e.g., $dS/dt = -\beta SI$). While computationally simple, these models assume that any individual is equally likely to contact any other, ignoring structural spatial environments (e.g. household clustering) and individual variances in daily routes.

To overcome these limitations, this project implements an Agent-Based Modeling (ABM) framework. The objective is to design and evaluate an executable system that bridges spatial mobility, virus exposure metrics, and government policy thresholds. By defining distinct physical structures (homes, offices, recreation hubs) and routing agents through daily schedule vectors, we model infectious spread at the exact point of interaction, highlighting the localized emergence of "super-spreading" events.

2. System Design & Agent Formalisms

EpiSim-ABM represents the environment as a continuous spatial grid bounded by functional hubs. The population consists of $N = 1,000$ autonomous agents, each instantiated with a unique set of demographic and behavioral traits.

2.1 Agent Attributes

Each agent $A_i$ is defined by a multi-dimensional state tuple:

$$A_i = \langle H_i, Age_i, M_i, C_{mask}, C_{lock}, H_{vac}, State_{health} \rangle$$
  • Household $H_i$: A assigned home coordinate cluster where the agent starts and ends the daily cycle.
  • Age Profile $Age_i$: Allocated via standard demographic distribution (10% Elderly, 60% Adults, 30% Youth). Age directly dictates the conditional probability of severe symptoms, hospitalization, and mortality upon infection.
  • Mobility Index $M_i$: Baseline speed and probability of commuting to optional recreational spots.
  • Mask Compliance $C_{mask}$: Bounded probability $[0, 1]$ that the agent wears a surgical mask in public settings.
  • Lockdown Adherence $C_{lock}$: Willingness to comply with home-quarantine orders during lockdown emergencies.
  • Vaccine Hesitancy $H_{vac}$: Probability threshold dictating whether the agent will accept or refuse an immunization dose from clinics.

2.2 Spatial Environment & Diurnal Scheduling

The space is partitioned into five distinct coordinate zone types: homes, workspaces, schools, recreational parks, and clinics. Agent commutes are governed by a global 24-hour synchronized clock:

  • 00:00 - 08:00 (Night): Agents reside in their designated Household coordinates. Local transmission can occur between co-habitants, simulating high household secondary attack rates.
  • 08:00 - 16:00 (Daytime Work/School): Work-active agents travel toward designated high-density commercial blocks, while youth agents move to school clusters. Densities in these blocks peak, modeling high occupational exposure.
  • 16:00 - 20:00 (Evening Leisure): Agents have set odds of commuting to recreation zones (public parks/shops), simulating random public contact.
  • 20:00 - 00:00 (Return home): Agents travel back to their household coordinates.

3. Health States & Viral Transmission Physics

The biological state transitions follow a modified SEIRD sequence:

Susceptible (S) Exposed (E)
Asymptomatic (I_A) Symptomatic (I_S)
Recovered (R) Deceased (D)

3.1 Exposure Formula

Transmission occurs spatially. At each time tick, for any susceptible agent $A_s$ located within physical distance $r$ of an active infectious agent $A_i$ (either symptomatic or asymptomatic), transmission occurs based on a daily probability vector:

$$P(Spread) = P_{base} \times (1 - \epsilon_{mask\_s} \cdot C_{mask\_s}) \times (1 - \epsilon_{mask\_i} \cdot C_{mask\_i})$$

where $P_{base}$ is the viral infectivity parameter, and $\epsilon_{mask}$ represents mask effectiveness (configured at 65%). If transmission is triggered, the agent moves from Susceptible (S) to Exposed (E) state, entering a latent non-infectious phase (incubation) for a configurable duration (default: 4-6 days).

3.2 Disease Progression & Hospitalization

Upon incubation completion, agents split into **Asymptomatic (I_A)** (30% probability, travels normally) or **Symptomatic (I_S)** (70% probability, speed reduced by 80%, quarantines if compliant). After 8-12 days of active infection, agents transition to **Recovered (R)** or experience severe health failure requiring hospital admission. Hospitalized agents are added to the global ICU queue. If ICU load exceeds capacity (100 beds), the conditional death rate for queued patients increases by a factor of 3x due to medical resource scarcity, modeling the emergent hazard of healthcare systems collapse.

4. Policy Simulation Experiments

Using the embedded Experimentation tab, we simulated the system under three policy structures to observe differences in epidemiological peak indices, total mortality, and system stress:

  1. Free Spread Scenario: Zero interventions active. This serves as the control cohort. Results show rapid explosive spread, high peak infection (exceeding 45% active cases simultaneously), and immediate hospital overflow, resulting in massive excess deaths.
  2. Dynamic Lockdown Scenario: Activated automatically when active symptomatic cases exceed 5% of the total population. Lockdown halts non-essential daily commutes, returning compliant agents to household boundaries. This results in the classic "Flattening the Curve" effect, keeping hospitalization under critical margins, although it prolongs the duration of the epidemic.
  3. High Vaccination Campaign: Vaccination clinics immunize 5 agents per day. Immunized agents have 95% protection against contracting the virus and 100% protection against severe hospitalization. This breaks the transmission chains entirely, suppressing the virus rapidly with negligible mortality.

5. Critical Evaluation & Academic Defense

While EpiSim-ABM demonstrates excellent explanatory power regarding micro-spatial clusters and the emergent non-linear benefits of rapid vaccinations, critical limitations exist:

  • Local Boundary Constraints: The grid acts as a closed ecosystem. In real-world policies, external seeding (imported travel cases) requires active screening at border thresholds, which our system omits.
  • Homogeneous Spacing Inside Zones: Although agents commute to office grids, their physical distribution inside the office boundary is modeled as random walking. Real offices feature office desks and meeting rooms that restrict contacts to localized subsets of coworkers.
  • Static Vaccine Hesitancy: In our model, vaccine hesitancy is static. In real epidemics, hesitancy is highly dynamic, fluctuating based on current infection numbers and media influence, creating a feedback loop between viral spread and willingness to seek vaccination.

Conclusion: EpiSim-ABM successfully shows that individual-level behaviors are crucial to epidemic outcomes. Rather than focusing solely on global rates, public health policies must protect high-density public zones and encourage local individual compliance to achieve lasting containment.
